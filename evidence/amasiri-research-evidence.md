# Amasiri Research — Evidence Snapshot

## Source Document

**AJUKA PROJECT ON SEDIMENTARY CHARACTERISTICS AND RESERVOIR POTENTIAL OF THE AMASIRI SANDSTONE**

The original research document contains **45 pages** of academic material covering the study background, geological setting, literature review, methodology, results, discussion, conclusion and references. fileciteturn28file0L1-L9

## Study Context

The research places the Amasiri Sandstone within the **Eze-Aku Group** of the Southern Benue Trough and identifies Amasiri in Afikpo North Local Government Area, Ebonyi State, Southeastern Nigeria. fileciteturn28file0L15-L30

The project states that its aim is to study the porosity and permeability of Amasiri sandstone as a basis for assessing reservoir potential, while also contributing descriptive detail, lithofacies identification, depositional-model interpretation, provenance, paleoclimate and tectonic-setting interpretation. fileciteturn28file0L44-L51

## Field Evidence

The documented field workflow consisted of:

1. Reconnaissance survey
2. Detailed geologic survey
3. Gridded-base-map and traverse mapping
4. Lithological observation and characterization
5. Representative sample collection
6. GPS recording of sample locations and elevations
7. Lithologic-log sketches
8. Measurement of bed attitudes and structures
9. Packaging, labelling and sealing of samples
10. Logging of lithology, texture and physical/biogenic sedimentary structures

The research states that lithofacies were defined from the combined primary features to support paleoenvironmental diagnosis. fileciteturn29file0L192-L209

## Laboratory Evidence

Six rock samples were collected for laboratory analysis of sedimentary characteristics and reservoir potential. fileciteturn29file0L212-L218

The documented methods include:

- Bulk density
- Particle density
- Total porosity
- Constant-head permeability

The porosity calculation is documented as:

`Total porosity (St) = 1 – Db/Dp`

where bulk density and particle density are used to estimate total porosity. fileciteturn29file0L219-L250

The constant-head permeability section documents the permeability relationship:

`k = QL/Aht`

with Q representing collected water volume, t time, h head causing flow, A sample cross-sectional area and L sample length. The source cites ASTM D2434 for the constant-head permeability method. fileciteturn29file0L253-L307

## Sample Descriptions & Coordinates

The project records GPS-supported sample locations and elevations. The descriptions include medium-coarse greyish-brown sandstone, fining-upward sandstone, sandstone showing erosional activity/breaks in depositional sequence, sandstone with conglomerates and poor grading, sandstone with a major depositional break, and coarse-grained brownish sandstone. fileciteturn29file0L353-L405

## Discussion Snapshot

The source discusses sorting, compaction and cementation as controls on porosity, and grain size/shape, fines, cement and fractures as factors affecting permeability. It reports a mean porosity of **39.63%**, a porosity range of **36.48%–43.70%**, and an empirical permeability mean of **0.0125 K cm/sec**. It also reports an approximate composition of 70% quartz, 25% feldspars and 5% clay minerals (mica) and calcite. fileciteturn29file0L406-L424

## Source Conclusion

The research concludes that the sandstones have a good average porosity value of **39.63%**, described by the project as excellent for potential reservoir quality, while the empirical permeability mean of **0.0125 K cm/sec** is interpreted in the source as indicating mineralogical immaturity. The project further states that low permeability may indicate isolated pore spaces in the studied samples. fileciteturn29file0L425-L441
